About ShipSafe

A free, no-signup check for the accessibility and privacy gaps that get websites demand letters — built for the wave of AI-generated and no-code sites.

Why it exists

AI builders and no-code tools made it genuinely easy to ship a professional-looking site in an afternoon. That's worth celebrating. But speed and compliance are different things, and most of these tools are optimized for the former.

The result is a predictable set of gaps: no lang attribute, images without alt text, unlabeled form fields, no privacy policy. These are the exact issues that automated ADA filers scan for — and they resulted in more than 4,600 website lawsuits in 2024 alone. The filings are often from the same small group of serial plaintiffs using the same scanning scripts looking for the same handful of missing attributes.

Most accessibility tools are built for developers and speak in audit codes. ShipSafe is built for the person who shipped the site and doesn't have a compliance background: here is your risk level, here is what to fix, here is why it matters — in plain English, in under 10 seconds.

Your privacy

The scan runs in your browser. ShipSafe fetches the target page through a public CORS proxy and analyzes the HTML client-side. We do not store your URL, log your scans, or track you across sessions. There is no account. The scan leaves no trace on our end.

The only network requests are: the fonts from Google Fonts (loaded once per browser, standard), and the CORS proxy fetches to read the URL you submitted. That proxy is a public service; its terms apply to it, not to ShipSafe.

Limitations

The free check reads your page's static HTML source — what the server returns before JavaScript runs. That is useful and catches a lot of real issues, but there are things it cannot see:

  • Color contrast is not checked. Measuring text-to-background contrast (WCAG 1.4.3) requires a rendered page, not source HTML. It is one of the more common accessibility failures and the free check does not catch it.
  • JavaScript-rendered content is not analyzed. If your site builds its interface after load — a React or Vue app, a Webflow interaction, a Squarespace section — the scan may see a near-empty HTML document. ShipSafe reports what it finds in the source, but what's there may not reflect the final page.
  • Only the URL you provide is scanned. ShipSafe does not crawl your other pages. Your homepage may look clean while a contact form on a subpage has unlabeled inputs.
  • Heuristics have limits. ShipSafe detects a privacy policy by matching link text or URLs against a pattern. A site can pass that check and still have an inadequate policy, or fail it with a valid policy under an unusual label.
  • This is heuristic guidance, not a legal opinion. A passing result means no obvious red flags in the source. It does not mean "certified accessible" or "legally compliant." For a binding assessment, consult a qualified accessibility professional or attorney.

A rendered-DOM scan — using a headless browser to analyze the page as a user would actually see it — is in development as the planned Pro tier. It will catch contrast, dynamic content, and JavaScript-built structure that the free check cannot reach.

The fine print

ShipSafe provides automated, heuristic guidance. It is not legal advice. It is not a certified WCAG conformance audit. It is not a guarantee of compliance or protection from legal action.
