Methodology

ShipSafe is deliberately transparent: here is exactly what it checks, how each check maps to a real standard, and how the grade is calculated. No black box.

What ShipSafe is — and isn't. It's a fast, automated heuristic that reads your page's HTML source and flags common, high-frequency risk areas in plain language. It is not legal advice, not a full WCAG conformance audit, and not a guarantee. It's the 10-second smoke test you run before — and instead of — ignoring the problem entirely.

The four categories

Each check returns pass, warn, fail, or not-applicable, and rolls up into a category score out of 100.

Accessibility (ADA / WCAG) — weighted highest

These are the issues automated ADA filers and overlay scanners look for first. Each maps to a Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2 success criterion:

Privacy & legal — weighted high

Accessibility complaints are frequently bundled with privacy claims. We check for a privacy-policy link, a cookie/consent mechanism (and flag when trackers load without one — a direct GDPR/CCPA exposure), a terms link, a contact path, and an accessibility statement (which signals good-faith effort and is shown to reduce demand-letter risk).

Schema & SEO · Trust signals — lighter weight

Structured-data validity, meta description, Open Graph, and canonical affect how you show up in search. HTTPS, favicon, viewport, and mixed-content are baseline trust checks. These don't carry legal risk, so they're weighted lower — but they're cheap wins.

How the grade is calculated

  • Each check scores pass = 1.0, warn = 0.5, fail = 0. Not-applicable checks are excluded.
  • A category score is the average across its checks, ×100.
  • Health is the mean of the four category scores; the letter grade is A (≥90), B (≥80), C (≥70), D (≥55), F (below).
  • Demand-letter risk is driven by the two legal categories: High if there are 3+ accessibility/privacy fails or either score is very low; Some if there's at least one legal fail; otherwise Low.

Honest limitations

  • It reads the HTML source, not the rendered page — so it can't measure color contrast or check content built entirely by JavaScript after load. A rendered scan is in development.
  • It uses heuristics (e.g. matching a "privacy" link by text/URL). A site can pass a heuristic and still have a problem, or vice-versa.
  • It is not a substitute for a manual audit or legal review. Treat a clean result as "no obvious red flags," not "certified compliant."
